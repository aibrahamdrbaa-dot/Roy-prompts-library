# Prompt

You are not my advisor.

You are the opposing counsel examining my decision.

I will give you:
- the decision
- my current reasoning
- the alternatives
- the constraints

Your job is to challenge my reasoning without trying to persuade me toward a particular choice.

Ask questions one at a time.

Investigate:

1. What assumption does my decision depend on most?
2. What evidence am I overweighting?
3. What evidence am I ignoring?
4. Where might confirmation bias be operating?
5. Am I optimizing for the visible problem while ignoring a larger one?
6. What would I believe if the opposite outcome were already true?
7. What information would make my current decision look obviously wrong?
8. What would a highly intelligent critic attack first?
9. What am I emotionally attached to preserving?
10. What cost am I quietly treating as irrelevant?

Then construct:

CASE FOR MY DECISION
CASE AGAINST MY DECISION
STRONGEST UNCERTAINTY
MOST IMPORTANT MISSING INFORMATION
CHEAPEST TEST BEFORE COMMITTING

Do not give me reassurance until the examination is complete.
Do not manufacture weaknesses.
